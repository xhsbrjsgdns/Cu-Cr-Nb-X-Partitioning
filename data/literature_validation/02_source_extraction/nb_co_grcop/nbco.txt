Phase equilibria in the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems were, respectively, determined by metallog-
raphy, X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy–energy dispersive X-ray (SEM–EDX)
techniques. The thermodynamic assessments of the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems were carried out
by using CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) method on the basis of the experimental data mea-
sured by the present and previous works. The Gibbs free energies of the liquid and solid solution phases
were described by the subregular solution model, and those of intermetallic compounds were described
by the sublattice model. A consistent set of the thermodynamic parameters has been obtained, and the
evaluated thermodynamic parameters lead to a better ﬁt between calculated results and experimental
data in both the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems.

1. Introduction
The Cu–Cr base alloys are important high-conductivity and
high-strength materials, such as the contact materials for medium-
voltage, high-vacuum interrupters [1–4]. Co and Nb are important
alloying elements to improve the properties of the dielectric
strength and corrosion resistance in the Cu–Cr base alloys, respec-
tively [5–8]. Knowledge of phase diagrams of the Cu–Cr–Nb and
Cu–Cr–Co systems is of importance to the development of the
high-conductivity and high-strength materials. However, the stud-
ies of phase equilibria in these systems are not adequate. Nikolayev
and Rozenberg [9] investigated the phase equilibria in the Cu-rich
corner of the Cu–Cr–Nb system, where the isothermal section dia-
grams at 873 and 1273 K in the Cu-rich portion containing up to
0.7 wt.% Nb and Cr were determined. Hasebe [10] experimentally
investigated several isothermal section diagrams at 1150–1550 K in
the Cu–Cr–Co alloys. In the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems, the
experimental data on thermodynamic properties were not avail-
able and thermodynamic assessments of phase equilibria were not
reported by the CALPHAD method, which is a powerful tool to
cut down on cost and time during development of materials [11].
Our goal is to develop a thermodynamic database of the phase

The purpose of the present work is (1) to experimentally deter-

2. Experimental determination of phase equilibria
2.1. Experimental procedure
The samples were prepared by direct arc melting using the con-
stituent elements of copper (99.95 wt.%), chromium (99.98 wt.%),
cobalt (99.9 wt.%), and niobium (99.7 wt.%) under argon atmo-
sphere with non-consumable tungsten electrode on a water-cooled
copper hearth. The overall weight loss is generally less than 1 wt.%.
The prepared alloy ingots were cut into small pieces by wire-
cutting machine. All specimens were sealed in quartz capsules
under vacuum and treated at 1373 K for 1 week for homogeniza-
tion, and were equilibrated at various temperatures from 1073 to
1573 K for several hours to 6 months, and then quenched into iced
water.
Microstructural examination of specimens after heat treatment
was carried out by optical microscopy. Two kinds of etchants were
used for metallographic examination in the different Cu–Cr–Nb
alloys to clearly show the microstructure after heat treatment.

It is interesting to ﬁnd that the solidiﬁed microstructures
of the Cu48Cr48Nb4, Cu47Cr47Nb6, Cu46Cr46Nb8, Cu30Cr30Nb40,
Cu30Cr38Nb32 and Cu30Cr50Nb20 (wt.%) alloys appear the separated

be found in the Cu–Cr–Co system. The separated macroscopic
morphology appears in the Cu46Cr8Co46 (wt.%) alloy, while the
homogenous macroscopic morphology appears in the Cu49Cr2Co49,
Cu48Cr4Co48 and Cu47Cr6Co47 (wt.%) alloys. Typical microstructures
are shown in Fig. 3(a) and (b). Such microstructures are similar with
the ones in the Cu–Fe base alloys, as reported in our previous work
[12,13].

3.2. Thermodynamic optimization
Several thermodynamic assessments of the Cu–Co [17–21],
Cu–Cr [22–26], Cu–Nb [22], Co–Cr [27–33], and Cr–Nb [34,35]
binary systems have been carried out by using the CALPHAD
method. The optimized parameters of the Cu–Co [17], Cu–Cr [22],
Cu–Nb [22], Co–Cr [27], and Cr–Nb [35] systems were used in the
present work by considering the consistency of the thermodynamic
database for the Cu-based alloys developed by our group [36–38].
The calculated binary phase diagrams are shown in Fig. 4.
The optimization was carried out by using the PARROT [39] mod-
ule in the Thermo-Calc software [40], which can handle various
kinds of experimental data. The experimental data of the phase
diagram were used as input to the program. Each piece of selected
information was given a certain weight by the importance of data,
and changed by trial and error during the assessment, until most
of the selected experimental information is reproduced within the
expected uncertainty limits. All the parameters were eventually
optimized to obtain the best consistency between calculated results
and experimental data. The evaluated ternary interaction parame-
ters for each phase in the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems are
listed in Table 3.
3.2.1. Phase equilibria in the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co ternary
systems

3.2.2. Miscibility gaps of the liquid phase in the Cu–Cr–Nb and
Cu–Cr–Co systems
It is well known that there exists a metastable miscibility gap
of the liquid phase at higher temperature in the Cu–Co [17,21,41],
Cu–Cr [23] and Cu–Nb [42] binary systems, respectively. According
to the present work, a stable miscibility gap of the liquid phase
appears when 4 wt.% Nb is added to the Cu–Cr system and 8 wt.%
Cr is added to the Cu–Co system.
The calculated metastable and stable miscibility gaps in the
liquid phase in the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co systems are shown
in Fig. 8. It can be seen that the tie-lines in the Cu–Cr–Nb and
Cu–Cr–Co systems lie along the Cu–CrNb and Cu–CoCr directions,
and the islands of miscibility gap exist in both systems. An expla-
nation for this is given from the predictions of Niessen et al. [43]
and the thermodynamic parameters used in the Cu–Cr–Nb and
Cu–Cr–Co systems summarized in Table 3. The relation between
mixing enthalpies of the liquid in these systems is as follows:
HCuNb(+) >> HCrCu(+) > HCrNb(−)
HCrCu(+) ≥HCoCu(+) > HCoCr(−)
Since there exists a repulsion force between Cu and Co, Nb, or
Cr, but an attractive force between Cr and Nb or Co, the tie-lines
radiate from the Cu corner to the Cr–Nb or Co–Cr sides.
4. Conclusions
(1) The phase equilibria were experimentally determined in the
Cu–Cr–Nb system and the Cu–Cr–Co system in the temperature
ranges from 1073 to 1573 K and from 1073 to 1373 K, respec-
tively. No ternary compound was found in both systems.
(2) Thermodynamic assessments in the Cu–Cr–Nb and Cu–Cr–Co
ternary phase diagrams were carried out based on the present
experimental values and available data from the literature by
the CALPHAD method. A good agreement between the calcu-
lated results and experimental data is obtained.
