A B S T R A C T   
The phase equilibria of the Cu–Cr–Si ternary system were investigated via thermodynamic modeling coupled 
with key experiments. Seventeen ternary alloys were prepared to determine the isothermal sections of the 
Cu–Cr–Si system at 700 and 800 ◦C by combining X-ray diffraction (XRD) and scanning electron microscopy with 
energy dispersive X-ray spectroscopy (SEM/EDS). No ternary compound was found. The solubilities of the third 
element in binary compounds in the Cu–Si and Cr–Si systems were measured. Based on the thermodynamic 
descriptions of three constitutive binary systems available in the literature as well as the experimental phase 
equilibria data obtained from the present work and literature, thermodynamic re-assessment of the Cu–Cr–Si 
system was carried out by the CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) approach. The substitutional model 
and sublattice model were used to describe the solution phases and intermediate phases, respectively. A set of 
thermodynamic parameters of the Cu–Cr–Si system was obtained. The calculated isothermal sections and vertical 
section are in good agreement with most of the reliable experimental data. The liquidus projection and reaction 
scheme of the Cu–Cr–Si system over the whole composition were also presented.   
1. Introduction 
Copper and copper alloys, which show good electrical conductivity, 
thermal conductivity, corrosion resistance and weldability etc., have 
been extensively utilized in various fields, such as resistance welding 
electrode, lead frames and contact wire for electrified railways [1–3]. 
High strength and high conductivity are the most important perfor­
mance requirements for copper alloys. However, the strength of pure 
copper is low, which can be improved by adding alloy elements. In 
Cu-based alloys, Cr and Si as two of the common additional elements 
improve the strength, corrosion resistance and thermoelectricity of 
copper alloys. Cu–Cr based alloys demonstrate the high strength and 
excellent conductivity and have been widely studied [4–6]. In addition, 
Si can make the face-centered cubic (fcc) phase of Cu more plastic, 
which is easy to work during cold deformation due to the large number 
of slip bands in the crystals [7]. The design of compositions of Cu alloys 
requires information about the phase equilibria of the Cu–Cr–Si ternary 
system. Therefore, a thorough thermodynamic assessment of the 
Cu–Cr–Si system is necessary for providing a set of reliable thermody­
namic parameters for thermodynamic extrapolations to related higher 
order systems. 
Thermodynamic description for the Cu–Cr–Si system is part of our 
efforts [8–11] to establish thermodynamic database of multi-component 
Cu based alloys. To the best of our knowledge, so far no complete 
thermodynamic parameter for the Cu–Cr–Si system is available in the 
literature. The purposes of the present work are to determine some 
representative isothermal sections of the Cu–Cr–Si system by key ex­
periments, and to obtain a set of complete thermodynamic parameters 
for the Cu–Cr–Si ternary system by the CALPHAD approach [12,13].

work from Chen et al. [25], which was accepted in the present work.

adopted in the present optimization. Yu et al. [32] studied the ther­
modynamic and kinetic of the liquid phase separation in the Cu–Cr–Si 
ternary system and determined the isothermal sections at 900, 1000, 
1100 and 1200 ◦C annealed for 90, 60, 30 and 15 days by means of XRD 
and electron probe microanalyzer (EPMA). There were three 
three-phase regions, i.e., liquid + (Si) + CrSi2, liquid + CrSi2 + CrSi and 
liquid + CrSi + Cr5Si3, in the isothermal section at 900 ◦C, and the re­
lationships were the same to the isothermal sections at 1000, 1100 and 
1200 ◦C. The three-phase region, i.e., (Cu) + (Cr) + Cr3Si, in the 
isothermal sections at 900 and 1000 ◦C was changed into the 
three-phase region, i.e., liquid + (Cr) + Cr3Si, in the isothermal sections 
at 1100 ◦C and 1200 ◦C. Based on their experimental results, they 
thermodynamically assessed the Cu–Cr–Si system using the CALPHAD 
approach, and a set of thermodynamic parameters, which can reproduce 
well the phase equilibria at high temperature, was obtained. However, 
only the solubility of the CrSi2 phase was considered in the work of Yu 
et al. [32]. In addition, the phase equilibria of the Cu–Cr–Si system at

3. Experimental procedures 
In order to determine the phase equilibria of the Cu–Cr–Si system at 
700 and 800 ◦C, seventeen ternary alloys were prepared by melting 
mixture of the high purity Cu (99.99 wt.%) rods, Cr (99.99 wt.%) blocks 
and Si (99.999 wt.%) blocks (China New Metal Materials Technology 
Co., Ltd.) as starting materials. The nominal compositions of the samples 
were listed in Table 2. The alloy samples, each with a weight of 
approximately 1 g, were prepared in a non-consumable vacuum arc- 
melting furnace (WK–I, Physcience Opto-electronics Co., Ltd. Beijing, 
China) with water-cooled copper crucible under 99.999 % pure Ar at­
mosphere. Each alloy was re-melted at least five times to improve their 
homogeneities. The alloys after arc melting were not subjected to 
chemical analysis since the weight losses were less than 1 wt.% for all 
alloys. Then the samples were sealed in evacuated quartz capsules with 
vacuum sealing machine (MRVS-1002, Wuhan Bailibo Technology Co., 
Ltd., China), and annealed at 700 ◦C (30 days) and 800 ◦C (25 days) in 
the high temperature diffusion furnace (KSL-1200X, Hefei Kejing Ma­
terial Technology Co., Ltd., China), followed by quenching in cold 
water. 
The powder XRD measurement of the annealed alloys was performed 
using a Cu-Kα radiation at 40 kV and 300 mA (Smartlab SE, Rigaku 
corporation, Japan) at room temperature. Diffraction patterns were 
generally acquired in a scan step 0.02◦of over a 2θ range 20–80◦. Then 
the samples were further examined using scanning electron microscopy 
equipped with energy dispersive X-ray analysis (SEM/EDS) (accelera­
tion voltage 20 kV, scanning speed 10 μs, working distance 4.5 mm) 
(JSM-6360LV/GENESIS2000XM60, JEOL, Japan), which the equipment 
standard uncertainty is 0.05 at.%. Generally, three points of each phase 
were measured by EDS analysis and their average value was taken as the 
phase composition. The phase composition wasn’t calibrated to the 
standard sample and the error range of the average value compared to 
the compositions of each points was about 1 at.%. 
4. Thermodynamic model 
The Gibbs energy functions of the pure elements Cu, Cr and Si were 
taken from the SGTE database compiled by Dinsdale [33]. The ther­
modynamic parameters of the Cu–Si, Cu–Cr and Cr–Si binary systems 
reported by Yan and Chang [17], Liu et al. [21] and Chen et al. [25] were 
accepted in the present work. 
The phases in the Cu–Cr–Si system to be optimized in the present 
work are as follows: solution phases, i.e., liquid, fcc (Cu), bcc (Cr), 
diamond (Si), hcp(CuSi) and bcc(CuSi); eight binary phases extending 
into the ternary system, i.e., Cu19Si6, Cu15Si4, Cu56Si11, Cu33Si7, CrSi2, 
CrSi, αCr5Si3 and Cr3Si.

5.2. Thermodynamic calculation results and discussion 
Based on the experimental data obtained from the present work and 
literature, the thermodynamic parameters of the Cu–Cr–Si system were 
evaluated by the optimization module PARROT [37,38] of the program 
Thermo-Calc, which works by minimizing the square sum of the dif­
ferences between measured and calculated values. The step-by-step 
optimization procedure [39–41] was utilized in the present assessment. 
The optimization starts with the isothermal section at 700 ◦C. Firstly, 
according to the phase equilibria and the solubilities of Cu and Cr in the 
binary phases in the Cu–Si and Cr–Si systems, respectively, the inter­
action parameters a for these phases in Eq. (2) were optimized. Then, the 
isothermal sections at 800, 900, 1000, 1100 and 1200 ◦C are considered 
one by one in the optimization process. Next, the ternary interaction 
parameters A and B for the phase liquid in Eq. (1) are obtained by fitting 
the experimental data of invariant reactions. Finally, all the parameters 
are optimized simultaneously. The thermodynamic parameters obtained 
in the present work are listed in Table 3. 
The calculated isothermal sections of the Cu–Cr–Si system at 700 and 
800 ◦C compared with the experimental phase equilibria data obtained 
in the present work and the isothermal sections at 900, 1000, 1100 and 
1200 ◦C compared with the experimental data reported from literatures 
were presented in Fig. 4 (a)–(f), respectively. It can be seen that the 
calculated isothermal sections at 700 and 800 ◦C are in good agreement 
with our experiment data. Similarly, the calculated isothermal sections 
at 900, 1000, 1100 and 1200 ◦C are in reasonable agreement with most 
of the reliable experimental data from the work of Yu et al. [32].

describing ternary and higher order systems. 
6. Conclusions  
● The phase equilibria of the Cu–Cr–Si system at 700 and 800 ◦C have 
been experimentally investigated by means of the XRD and SEM/EDS 
methods. Six three-phase regions and two two-phase regions at 
700 ◦C, and six three-phase regions and three two-phase regions at 
800 ◦C were determined. No ternary compound was found in the 
ternary system. The solubilities of Cr in Cu–Si compounds and the 
solubilities of Cu in Cr–Si compounds were measured and their 
values are less than 4 at.% in general.  
● The thermodynamic assessment of the Cu–Cr–Si systems was carried 
out by using the CALPHAD method based on the experimental data 
available in the present work and literature. A set of complete 
thermodynamic parameters of the Cu–Cr–Si system was obtained. 
The calculated isothermal sections and vertical section are in good 
agreement with most of the reliable experimental data. The liquidus 
projection and reaction scheme of the Cu–Cr–Si system over the 
whole composition were also presented.
